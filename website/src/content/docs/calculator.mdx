Enter how many minutes of 4-vCPU Linux jobs you run a month. The table shows
what they cost on GitHub-hosted runners and on backmesh's spot VMs in each
cloud. backmesh itself is free; the backmesh rows are what your cloud bills.

## Where the numbers come from

The figures are the same ones the landing page uses, and
[Cost](/cost/) shows how they're computed, with every input and its date.
Your GitHub minutes are on your org's billing page, under Actions usage.

Two things move the backmesh range. Spot prices change by the hour, and the
range spans the VM types a job may get. Shorter jobs pay for more VM boots per
minute of work. Each job pays its VM's boot, the measured median from the
kernel's start to the runner's container start:
{per1000.map((p) => `${p.bootS} s on ${p.cloud}`).join(', ')}
([vm-boot-marks](https://github.com/anycloud-sh/anycloud/tree/main/experiments/vm-boot-marks#result)).
The [benchmarks](/benchmarks/) have the whole wait for a VM on AWS, from
the job's queueing.

The calculator leaves out GitHub's included usage, which is $18 a month on
Team and $300 on Enterprise Cloud. Subtract it from the GitHub-hosted row if
your org uses it on these jobs.

GitHub announced, then postponed, a $0.002 per minute fee for self-hosted
runners. If it returns, add $0.002 per minute to the backmesh rows; see
[GitHub's self-hosted runner fee](/blog/github-self-hosted-runner-fee/).
