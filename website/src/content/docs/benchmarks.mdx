We build five pinned open-source projects on GitHub's `ubuntu-latest` and on
backmesh's `backmesh-ubuntu-2404-4vcpu` spot VMs on AWS, {bench.rounds} rounds each, and on
`backmesh-ubuntu-2404-od-4vcpu` on-demand VMs in {bench.odRounds} rounds alongside them. These
are the latest results, from {bench.date} on backmesh {bench.version}. The
figures are medians, and every run is [linked below](#runs).

The [build benchmark](https://github.com/backmesh/backmesh/tree/main/benchmarks/builds)
has every result since 2026-11-22, the warm runs, each label's VM types,
Mann-Whitney p for each speed, and where the wait goes.

## What the headline figures measure

On GitHub, the time a job takes is queued to done: its wait for a runner, then
its steps. The headline's speed figures time the steps alone, first step to
done, so each has its queued-to-done figure beside it.

## Method

- **Time** is a job's first step to its end, as GitHub records them.
- **Wait** is a job's queueing to its first step. backmesh's runners started
  without a cue, so each job waited for its VM to boot. With
  [`backmesh cue`](/#cue), the runner boots before the push: see the
  [cue benchmark](https://github.com/backmesh/backmesh/tree/main/benchmarks/cue#results).
- **Size.** [backmesh/benchmarks](https://github.com/backmesh/benchmarks) is
  public, so `ubuntu-latest` has 4 vCPUs, as `backmesh-ubuntu-2404-4vcpu` has, with 16 GiB.
- **GitHub's cost.** `ubuntu-latest` is free in a public repository. In a
  private one, a 4-vCPU GitHub-hosted runner costs ${GITHUB_4_CORE_PER_MIN} a
  minute, so each job's start to its end is rounded up to whole minutes at
  that price.
- **backmesh's cost** is each job's VM from its kernel's start to the job's
  end, at least a minute, as AWS bills it, at the VM family's price from the
  [cost page](/cost/)'s {CATALOG_DATE} catalog: spot on `backmesh-ubuntu-2404-4vcpu`
  ({prices(AWS_SPOT_XLARGE)} an hour), on demand on `backmesh-ubuntu-2404-od-4vcpu`
  ({prices(AWS_ON_DEMAND_XLARGE)}). Its disk, 100 GB of gp3 at 4,000 IOPS
  and 500 MiB/s, adds about ${AWS_DISK_PER_HOUR} an hour. It leaves out
  backmesh's always-on control plane, on the [cost page](/cost/).
- **CPUs.** `backmesh-ubuntu-2404-od-4vcpu`'s jobs landed on {landed(bench.odFamilies)}, and
  `backmesh-ubuntu-2404-4vcpu`'s on {landed(bench.spotFamilies)}. An `m8a`'s or `m7a`'s vCPUs
  are full cores; an `m8i`'s are hyperthreads. The
  [build benchmark](https://github.com/backmesh/backmesh/tree/main/benchmarks/builds#results)
  lists them, and `ubuntu-latest`'s CPUs.
- **Ratios** compare the unrounded medians, to one decimal, and within 10%
  read as fast; the headline rounds the largest down to one significant figure.
- **Caches.** Each cold run is on a new branch, with no caches. No step
  caches anything: `setup-node`'s, `setup-go`'s and `setup-uv`'s own caches
  are off.
- **Toolchains.** `ubuntu-latest` has Rust preinstalled. backmesh's image
  doesn't: it installs Rust from a pinned release when the job first calls
  it, inside the measured time
  ([backmesh#124](https://github.com/backmesh/backmesh/pull/124)).

## The workflow

[`benchmarks/builds/bench.yml`](https://github.com/backmesh/backmesh/blob/main/benchmarks/builds/bench.yml)
has a job for each build, and
[`run.ts`](https://github.com/backmesh/backmesh/blob/main/benchmarks/builds/run.ts)
pushes it to a new branch of
[backmesh/benchmarks](https://github.com/backmesh/benchmarks) for each round,
with the label for `runs-on`.
